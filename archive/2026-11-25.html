Stocks and your savings

Chip stocks slump from New York to Seoul as AI doubts return.

High stakes for savers

Happened

On Tuesday the S&P 500 fell 0.50% to 7,201.09, its eighth loss in nine sessions, and the Nasdaq fell 1.48% to 23,733.54. The Dow rose 0.85% to 50,018.70. The Philadelphia Semiconductor Index dropped 7.9%. Nvidia fell 5.86%, Micron 11.01%, Intel 8.15% and AMD 8.86%, while Kohl's, Dollar Tree and Dick's Sporting Goods jumped after earnings.

Happening

On Wednesday Asia followed: the KOSPI fell 6.2%, the Taiex 5%, the Nikkei 2.8% to 57,011.62 and the Shanghai Composite 1.5% to its lowest in more than a year, AP reports. Samsung fell 9.9%, SK Hynix 11.1% and SoftBank Group 14.8%. India's Sensex rose 2.4%. Europe's STOXX 600 was down 0.7% in early trade and the DAX 1.3%, Saxo says. US markets close Thursday for Thanksgiving.

Could happen

Two days of 30% and 6% swings in one stock show how much of the market now rides on a handful of AI names. Wednesday's PCE report adds a second test.

FIOPO read. Judgment, not fact.

What it means for you

  • Check how much of your retirement fund sits in the ten largest tech stocks; an S&P 500 fund is more concentrated than it looks.
  • Keep regular contributions on schedule rather than reacting to single day swings.
  • Money you need within two years belongs in savings or short Treasury bills, not in chip stocks.

Trade and tariffs

The US is preparing new tariffs on China, the EU and 14 other economies.

High stakes for prices

Happened

The Trump administration is expected to soon announce higher tariffs on 16 economies, including China and the EU, two people familiar with the matter told AP on Tuesday. The US Trade Representative is due to release results of monthslong Section 301 investigations into industrial subsidies in the coming days. Bloomberg and The Wall Street Journal reported the plans earlier.

Happening

The tariff levels were not clear and timing could still change, AP says. The White House says subsidized overproduction in those countries floods the US with cheap goods. The duties would come on top of 10% to 12.5% tariffs announced this year, and would replace broader April 2025 tariffs the Supreme Court struck down in February. The White House and USTR did not respond to AP. Trump met Xi Jinping in Shenzhen last week, and Xi is expected at the G20 in Florida next month.

What it means for you

  • Planning a big purchase of imported goods, such as appliances or electronics? Prices can rise once new tariffs take effect.
  • Run a small business that imports? Ask suppliers now how they would share any new duty.
  • Own funds with heavy exposure to exporters in China or Europe? Expect swings when the list is published.

The Fed and your loans

Consumer confidence jumps to 103.3 as traders bet on a December Fed hike.

High stakes for borrowers

Happened

The Conference Board's consumer confidence index rose 11.4 points to 103.3 in November from 91.9, well above the 92.2 forecast. The expectations index rose to 83.6 and 12 month inflation expectations eased to 5.7%. Conference Board economist Fergus McCormick linked the gain to lower uncertainty after the midterm elections, while consumers still complained about high prices.

Happening

Futures put the odds of a quarter point Fed hike on December 9 at about 95% to 96%, with the rest on a half point, according to CME FedWatch data cited by Closing Look. The 10 year Treasury yield closed Tuesday at 4.93% and eased to 4.875% early Wednesday, Saxo says. Australia's October inflation came in at 4.9%, above the 4.7% forecast. US PCE inflation, income and spending, jobless claims and durable goods all arrive Wednesday.

Deals and health care

Sanofi agrees to buy Insmed for about $41 billion in cash.

Medium stakes for investors

Happened

France's Sanofi agreed on November 24 to buy US drugmaker Insmed for $175 a share in cash, a total equity value of about $41.0 billion. The deal brings Brinsupri, a once daily pill for the lung disease non cystic fibrosis bronchiectasis, which Sanofi says earned $1.09 billion through September 30, 2026, a little over a year after its US launch.

Happening

Both boards approved the deal, which needs Insmed shareholder and regulatory approval and is expected to close in the first half of 2027. Sanofi will fund it with debt and cash and expects it to add to earnings per share by the end of 2028. Insmed shares rose 14.13% on Tuesday, and Sanofi's shares were up 9.8% in Paris on Wednesday morning, Saxo reports.

Could happen

A deal this size draws close review from regulators, but the two companies have little overlap in respiratory drugs.

FIOPO read. Judgment, not fact.

What it means for you

  • Own Insmed shares? A cash offer usually caps the upside; read the shareholder vote materials when they arrive.
  • Taking Brinsupri? Ownership changes rarely affect prescriptions, but ask your pharmacy about any change in patient support programs.
  • Own a health care fund? Big pharma deals can lift other mid size biotech stocks on takeover hopes.
Watch: The Insmed shareholder vote date.Sanofi press release (SEC filing), Nov 24, Closing Look, Nov 24, Saxo Market Quick Take, Nov 25

South America

Argentina's peso hits its weakest close in a year as Brazil's spending bill heads to Lula.

The dollar closed at 1,592 pesos on Tuesday, the peso's weakest close in 12 months, after September economic activity fell 1.2% from a year earlier, The Rio Times reports. For you: Traveling to Argentina? A weaker peso stretches dollars further, but check card fees and official exchange rates before you go.
