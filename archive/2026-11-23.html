Stocks and your savings

Nvidia rehires Jensen Huang as stocks try to bounce from the worst week since 2020.

High stakes for savers

Happened

The S&P 500 fell 3.7% on Friday to 6,910.39 and 8.6% for the week, entering a correction. The Dow fell 1.93% to 48,147.33 and 8% for the week, its worst since March 2020. The Nasdaq dropped 5.28% to 22,617.73 and is now down 2.7% for 2026. Nvidia fell about 14% on Friday. The VIX fear gauge closed at 35.89, its highest since April 2025.

Happening

On Monday Nvidia's board voted unanimously to reinstate Jensen Huang as president, CEO and board director, effective immediately, 11 days after he resigned on November 12. The board said input from senior levels of the US government gave it confidence that his return poses no national security risk. An independent board investigation will continue, a new National Security and Export Control Committee of independent directors will oversee those risks, and Colette Kress returns to her role as CFO. Earlier, after President Trump urged the board on Truth Social to rehire Huang, Nvidia rose 8.69% to $136.44 in premarket trading at 6:35 a.m. ET. By 7:53 a.m. ET, S&P 500 futures were up 2.60% and Nasdaq 100 futures 3.49%, Yahoo Finance showed. On Sunday night Dow futures rose 798 points, or 1.66%, S&P 500 futures 2.11% and Nasdaq futures 2.75%. The 10 year Treasury yield was about 4.94%. Gold rose 2.62% to $3,607.55 on Monday after falling about 9% last week. US markets close Thursday for Thanksgiving and close early on Friday.

Could happen

One strong morning does not end a correction. Wednesday's PCE inflation report and the Fed's December 9 decision will shape whether the selling resumes.

FIOPO read. Judgment, not fact.

What it means for you

  • Investing for retirement? Keep your regular contributions going; selling after an 8.6% week locks in the loss.
  • Need cash in the next year or two? Keep it in savings or short Treasury bills, not stocks.
  • Own an S&P 500 or Nasdaq fund? Nvidia is one of its largest weights, so a big Nvidia day moves your balance too. Check your exposure before you add more.

Cybersecurity deal

CrowdStrike agrees to buy Zscaler for about $43.9 billion in stock.

Medium stakes for investors

Happened

CrowdStrike and Zscaler announced a definitive all-stock deal on Monday, November 23. Zscaler holders get 1.135 CrowdStrike shares for each share, about $260.41 based on Friday's close, a premium of about 34%. The enterprise value is about $43.9 billion. Bloomberg first reported talks on November 20.

Happening

In premarket trading on Monday, CrowdStrike fell 9.6% and Zscaler rose 13%, Yahoo Finance reported. CrowdStrike holders would own about 84.1% of the combined company and Zscaler holders 15.9%. Zscaler founder Jay Chaudhry, whose group holds about 34% of the shares, signed voting agreements and will join CrowdStrike's board. The firms target about $1 billion a year in extra revenue within 30 months and $500 million in cost savings, with combined annual recurring revenue above $10 billion.

Could happen

The deal needs Zscaler shareholder and regulatory approval and is expected to close in the second half of CrowdStrike's fiscal 2028, so a long review is likely.

FIOPO read. Judgment, not fact.

What it means for you

  • Own either stock? Read the proxy when it arrives; you will be asked to vote or will receive new shares.
  • Work in cybersecurity? Mergers this size often bring team changes, so keep your skills and contacts current.
  • Business customer of both? Ask your account rep how pricing and contracts change after the merger.

Innovation and disruption

What is changing jobs, prices or daily life, explained plainly.

Batteries

A new cathode design could stop batteries losing voltage over time.

Researchers reported in Nature Sustainability on November 20 that lithium-rich cathodes with an O6-type layered structure suppress the voltage decline that limits these high-capacity materials.

For you: Thinking about an EV? Battery life keeps improving, so compare warranty terms on range loss.

Everything Science, Nov 22

Cleanup

Bacteria removed about 98% of a radioactive metal in 60 hours.

A study in Nature Communications on November 19 found the microbe Methylacidiphilum fumariolicum SolV removed about 98% of promethium-147 from its growth medium over 60 hours, a possible tool for cleaning nuclear waste.

For you: Work in environmental services? Biological cleanup is a growing field to watch for jobs and contracts.

Everything Science, Nov 22

AI and health

Routine blood tests could flag cancer complications early.

A model in npj Digital Medicine on November 17 uses how routine lab results change over time to predict organ complications in cancer patients. It has not yet been shown to reduce complications.

For you: Being treated for cancer? Ask your care team how they track trends in your lab results, not just single readings.
