Oil nears $97 after reported mine hits on two tankers and a strike on a Saudi refinery.

Every story on FIOPO is traced to where it lands: your fuel, your rent, your savings, your job. Here is how today's biggest story travels.

1

The event

2 tankers

Iran's Revolutionary Guards said on November 26 that two oil tankers hit naval mines and caught fire north of Oman's port of Khasab in the Strait of Hormuz. It said one was a supertanker carrying more than 2 million barrels. The account comes from the IRGC and was reported by Iran International.

2

The second hit

Jazan

The same day, Saudi Arabia's Energy Ministry said a hostile aerial projectile hit the Jazan refinery, which can process up to 400,000 barrels a day, starting a fire and suspending some operations. One Nepali worker was killed and several others were injured, Arab News reports.

3

The price

Brent $97.31

Brent rose 0.77% to $97.31 a barrel in Asian trade on Friday and US crude rose 2.99% to $86.18, AP reports. Brent settled Thursday at its highest since November 6. On Wednesday Brent closed at $90.82.

4

The ripple

Sensex -1.17%

India, a big oil importer, felt it first: the Sensex fell 989.33 points to 83,339.26 on Friday and the rupee weakened to about 92.98 per dollar, with rising crude the main drag, IANS reports. ANZ says Brent can push above $100 even without another major escalation.

5

You

Gas $3.56

US gas averaged $3.56 on November 26, down from $3.72 a week earlier, AAA says. Higher crude, if it lasts, could slow that decline. OPEC+ meets Sunday to set 2027 output policy.

Oil and the Gulf

Oil climbs as Iran's IRGC says two tankers hit mines in Hormuz and a strike shuts part of a Saudi refinery.

High stakes for fuel prices

Deals and companies

Altria says it may make a cash offer for Imperial Brands, and Imperial's shares jump 13.3%.

Medium stakes for investors

Happened

On November 26 Altria, the Marlboro maker in the US, said it is considering a possible offer for Britain's Imperial Brands that would likely be solely in cash, and that it is talking with a third party about buying part of Imperial's business. Imperial said it has had no discussions with and received no approach from Altria or the third party. Imperial shares rose 13.3% to 3,432 pence, their highest close since July 2017, Investing.com reports.

Happening

Bloomberg reported that Japan Tobacco has explored partnering with another buyer and splitting Imperial's assets by region, at an early stage, Investing.com says. Japan Tobacco shares closed up 4.5% on Friday against a 1.7% rise in the TOPIX, Reuters reports. Imperial is valued at £25.90 billion ($34 billion), and Altria had $2.7 billion in cash at the end of September. Under UK takeover rules Altria must make a firm offer or walk away by 5 p.m. on December 24.

Could happen

A deal of this size would be the largest takeover of a listed UK company in more than eight years, but nothing has been offered yet.

FIOPO read. Judgment, not fact.

What it means for you

  • Own Imperial shares? Imperial has told shareholders to take no action for now.
  • Hold Altria for its dividend? Watch Friday's first US trade on the news and any detail on how a deal would be paid for.
  • Hold a UK or global dividend fund? Check whether Imperial is a large holding after the jump.

Central banks and your loans

EU leaders pick Spain's Pablo Hernández de Cos to lead the ECB as Tokyo inflation hits a 16 month high.

Medium stakes for borrowers

Happened

At a summit that opened on November 26, EU leaders agreed to nominate Pablo Hernández de Cos, former governor of Spain's central bank and head of the Bank for International Settlements, as the next ECB president, Reuters and AFP report. He would be the first Spaniard in the job. France's Laurence Boone was nominated to run market operations and Germany's Markus Brunnermeier as chief economist. Christine Lagarde said in October she will step down on February 7, 2027.

Happening

The European Council will consult the European Parliament and the ECB Governing Council, then formally appoint all three at its December meeting, LB.ua reports. The same summit ended without a deal on the 2028 to 2034 EU budget, Eunews says. In Japan, Tokyo core inflation rose to 2.9% in November, a 16 month high but below the 3.0% forecast. The Bank of Japan, at 1.25%, meets December 17 to 18, and markets expect a hike to 1.5%.

Could happen

A new ECB leadership seen as continuity, a likely Japanese hike and a likely US Fed hike on December 9 point to borrowing costs staying high into 2027.

FIOPO read. Judgment, not fact.

What it means for you

  • Have a variable rate loan in euros or yen? Ask your lender how a December rate change would affect your payment.
  • Holding cash? Compare savings and certificate rates, which tend to rise after hikes.
  • Traveling to Japan? The yen is near 160 per dollar, investingLive reports, so compare card and exchange fees.

Medicine

The FDA approves the first new hormone therapy for early breast cancer in more than 20 years.

Genentech's giredestrant, sold as Wilserdly, was approved on November 23 as an after surgery treatment for stage I to III ER positive, HER2 negative breast cancer at intermediate to high risk of recurrence. In the lidERA trial of 4,170 adults it cut the risk of invasive recurrence by 30% versus standard hormone therapy, with about 92% alive and cancer free after three years versus 89.6%.

For you: Being treated for early breast cancer? Ask your oncologist whether the new option fits and whether your insurer covers it.

Yahoo News, Nov 24

Physics

Physicists hold a single antimatter particle in its lowest quantum state for months.

A Northwestern team led by Gerald Gabrielse trapped one positron, the electron's antimatter twin, cooled it to about 300 millikelvin and held it for months, ScienceDaily reported on November 25. The team moved it between energy levels with microwaves. Next it plans to compare the positron's magnetism with the electron's, a test that could reveal physics beyond the Standard Model.

For you: Basic research like this is decades from products; it is worth following, not investing on.

ScienceDaily, Northwestern, Nov 25

Surgery and robotics

The largest trial of robotic knee replacement finds no clear benefit over standard surgery.

The UK RACER-Knee trial, published in The Lancet, compared robotic arm assisted surgery with conventional knee replacement in 339 patients at 10 hospitals and found no meaningful difference in a key one year patient score, ScienceDaily reported on November 25. Robotic surgery added about 10.5 minutes and roughly £950 per operation and was not cost effective in year one.

For you: Facing a knee replacement? Ask your surgeon about their own results; newer technology is not automatically better.

ScienceDaily, University of Warwick, Nov 25
