The US proposes new tariffs of 15% to 35% on China, the EU, India and 11 other economies.

Every story on FIOPO is traced to where it lands: your fuel, your rent, your savings, your job. Here is how today's biggest story travels.

1

The event

14 economies

On November 25 the US Trade Representative proposed Section 301 tariffs on 14 economies it says flood the US with subsidized goods, after investigations opened in March. Top proposed rates run from 15% to 35%.

2

The list

Vietnam 35%

Vietnam faces up to 35%, India up to 25%, China and Singapore up to 20%, and five Southeast Asian and South Asian economies up to 18%. The EU, Japan, Korea, Taiwan and Switzerland face up to 15%.

3

The reaction

STOXX +1.4%

China's commerce ministry called the probes "a typical act of unilateralism." Brazil called a separate 10% US tariff on its aircraft "unilateral and unacceptable." European stocks rose 1.4% Thursday on hopes EU duties stay capped at 15%, Reuters reports.

4

The timeline

Jan 25

Nothing takes effect yet. Comments are due January 5 and hearings start January 25. USTR Jamieson Greer told CNBC he did not expect tariffs earlier than "a couple months from now." China's rate could rise to 40% if the truce expiring January 10 fails.

Stocks and your savings

Wall Street erases a 1,100 point Dow drop, and Asia rebounds on Thursday.

High stakes for savers

Happened

On Wednesday the Dow closed up 0.33% at 50,182.95 after falling more than 1,100 points. The S&P 500 fell 0.25% to 7,182.78 after being down as much as 3.2%, and the Nasdaq fell 0.62% to 23,585.80 after a 4.6% intraday drop. Trade and tariffs

The US proposes tariffs of 15% to 35% on 14 economies, including China, the EU and India.

High stakes for prices

Happened

On November 25 the US Trade Representative found that industrial policies in 14 economies are actionable under Section 301 and proposed new tariffs. Proposed top rates: Vietnam 35%, India 25% (17% with a trade deal), China and Singapore 20%, Bangladesh, Cambodia, Indonesia, Malaysia and Thailand 18%, and the EU, Japan, Korea, Taiwan and Switzerland 15%. USTR made no finding on Mexico or Norway. It also proposed a 10% tariff on finished Brazilian aircraft.

Happening

The rates are proposals. Comments are due January 5 and hearings start January 25, 2027. Most economies get exemptions for goods already under Section 232 tariffs, such as cars, steel and chips. China's commerce ministry said it "firmly opposes" the plan and reserves the right to respond. Senator Ron Wyden said the tariffs would raise prices for Americans. European shares rose Thursday on hopes EU duties stay capped at the 15% agreed last year.

Could happen

Some take effect by Dec 2610%

FIOPO read for the next 30 days, given the January hearing schedule. Judgment, not fact.

What it means for you

  • Planning to buy furniture, clothing or electronics made in Vietnam, India or China? Prices may rise in 2027 if the rates are confirmed.
  • Run a business that imports from these economies? Ask your customs broker whether your goods fall under the exemption annexes, and note the January 5 comment deadline.
  • Hold funds focused on Southeast Asian or Indian exporters? Expect swings as the hearings approach.

The Fed and your loans

US core inflation hits 3.3% as Korea raises rates and a Fed hike looks near certain.

High stakes for borrowers

Happened

October PCE inflation rose 3.8% from a year earlier, in line with forecasts, and core PCE rose 3.3%, above the 3.2% consensus. Third quarter growth was revised up to a 4.3% annual pace from 3.4%. The Fed's Beige Book said eight of 12 districts reported growth, with prices still rising moderately. On Thursday the Bank of Korea raised its rate a quarter point to 3.25%, its third hike this year, in a 5 to 2 vote.

Happening

After the data, CME FedWatch put the odds of a quarter point Fed hike on December 9 at 97.8%, Closing Look reports. The 10 year Treasury yield fell 6 basis points to 4.85% and the 2 year to 4.61%. The average 30 year mortgage rate was 7.21%, The Rio Times reports. Bank of Korea Governor Shin Hyun-song called the move "both a preemptive move and a response to mounting inflationary pressures" and said more hikes remain on the table.

Oil and the Gulf

Yemen's Houthis say they struck Saudi Arabia's largest refinery at Ras Tanura.

Medium stakes for fuel prices

Happened

Houthi spokesman Yahya Saree said on November 25 that the group struck the Ras Tanura refinery, causing fires, and attacked Riyadh's financial district. The Saudi led coalition said air defenses destroyed four drones at Ras Tanura and did not say the refinery was hit. Saudi civil defense said 11 people were wounded on November 24 in a strike on a Riyadh tower. The National says the Houthi claims could not be independently verified.

Happening

Ras Tanura can process 550,000 barrels a day, about 16% of Saudi refining capacity, The National reports. US crude settled up 2% at $83.68 on Wednesday, and on Thursday Brent traded at $91.11, up 0.5%, and WTI at $84.33, AP says. Brent was about $72 before the US Iran war began in late February. OPEC+ meets November 29.

Could happen

Repeated strikes on Gulf energy sites keep a risk premium in oil prices even as US pump prices fall.

FIOPO read. Judgment, not fact.

What it means for you

  • Driving this weekend? US gas averaged $3.59 on November 25, AAA says, down from $3.72 a week earlier, so fill up where prices are posted lowest.
  • Heat with heating oil? Oil shocks reach heating oil quickly; compare suppliers before you refill.
  • Shipping goods through the Gulf? Ask your carrier about war risk surcharges.

European stocks jump 1.4% on hopes US tariffs on the EU stay at 15%.

For you: Hold European stocks? Banks and chip makers are leading the rebound; check your fund's weighting before adding more.

Medicine

The FDA approves the first systemic US treatment for vitiligo and two other drugs.

AbbVie's Rinvoq won approval for nonsegmental vitiligo in adults and patients 12 and older, the first systemic US therapy for the condition, announced November 23. BridgeBio's Welgemda was approved for a form of limb girdle muscular dystrophy, and Cogent's Ekitna for advanced GIST, a rare stomach cancer, PR Newswire's roundup says.

For you: Have one of these conditions? Ask your doctor whether a new option fits and whether your insurer covers it yet.

Batteries

Researchers build a rechargeable zinc air battery using vitamin B12.

Researchers at Spain's University of Córdoba used vitamin B12 bound to carbon nanotubes as a catalyst in a rechargeable zinc air battery that completed up to 250 charge cycles, ScienceDaily reported on November 18. The work, published in the Journal of Materials Chemistry A, remains at the research stage.

For you: Cheaper, safer battery materials take years to reach stores; do not pay a premium for products claiming this technology today.

ScienceDaily, University of Córdoba, Nov 18
