Anthropic's record $81 billion IPO trades today. Here is how it reaches your wallet.

Every story on FIOPO is traced to where it lands: your fuel, your rent, your savings, your job. Here is how today's biggest story travels.

1

The event

$81B

Anthropic priced its IPO at $83 a share on Wednesday, above its $70 to $78 range. It is the largest listing on record. Shares start trading on the Nasdaq today as ANT.

2

The valuation

$1.5T

That is the fully diluted value at $83 a share, up from $965 billion in its private round in May. Revenue was $17.0 billion from July to September, more than 12 times a year earlier.

3

Big holders

AMZN +3.82%

Amazon owns 14.7% of Anthropic and Google 13.7%. Amazon rose 3.82% on Wednesday as the pricing neared.

4

Index funds

Nasdaq +1.83%

Nasdaq 100 futures rose 1.83% early Thursday after the S&P 500 fell for a fifth straight day. Chip stocks led, with Intel, AMD and Micron up 3% to 4.5% before the open.

5

You

6% float

Only about 6% of Anthropic's shares trade publicly, and up to 20% of the offering went to individual investors. A small float means big first day swings. Most savers already hold a slice through Amazon and Google in their index funds.

AI and your retirement account

Anthropic debuts today after the biggest IPO ever. Here is what to know before you click buy.

Medium stakes for investors

Happened

Anthropic sold 825 million shares at $83, raising $81 billion at a value of about $1.5 trillion. Google, Nvidia, AMD, Broadcom, Micron, Microsoft and TSMC also agreed to buy $26 billion of non-voting shares at the IPO price.

Happening

Trading starts on the Nasdaq as ANT once the lead banks are ready, which can be hours after the open. Up to 315 million insider shares may be sold from day one. Most other holders are locked up for up to 180 days, some for up to a year.

Could happen

Demand was strong enough to price above the range, but the float is small and the company lost $567 million from operations last quarter. Expect a volatile first week and another test when lockups end.

FIOPO read. Judgment, not fact. Not investment advice.

What it means for you

  • Want to buy on day one? Use a limit order so you do not pay far above what you intended in the opening rush.
  • Size any purchase as money you can afford to see drop by a third.
  • Own an S&P 500 or Nasdaq fund? You already have exposure through Amazon and Google, the two largest outside holders.
Watch: the first trade time and where ANT closes versus $83.TechCrunch, StockTitan, Reuters via Yahoo Finance
Medicare and health insurers

Medicare proposes tougher audits of private plans. UnitedHealth fell 10%.

Medium stakes for seniors and savers

Happened

CMS proposed the CRUSH rule on November 16. It would extend audits of Medicare Advantage billing back to payment year 2018. CMS estimates about $9 billion a year in recovered overpayments and nearly $90 billion in savings over 10 years.

Happening

UnitedHealth fell 10.21% to $349.91 on Wednesday and Humana 12.98%. Public comments are due January 19, 2027. It is a proposal, not a final rule.

Could happen

Insurers will push back during the comment period. If the rule holds, plans may trim extra benefits in later years to protect margins.

FIOPO read. Judgment, not fact.

What it means for you

  • On Medicare Advantage? This is only a proposal. Compare plans on benefits and costs before open enrollment ends December 7.
  • Own health insurer stocks directly? Check how much of your portfolio rides on one rule.
  • Want a say? Anyone can comment on the proposal until January 19.
Watch: insurer responses and whether CMS changes the rule after comments.FinChannel, The Motley Fool, Medicare.gov

Around the world

One story per region, weighted toward where most people live. Each ends with what it means for you.

China and East Asia

Japan's exports hit a record, but mostly on higher prices.

October exports rose 20.5% from a year earlier to 11.77 trillion yen, and imports 21.6% to 12.25 trillion yen. Export volume grew only 5.8%. It was Japan's sixth straight monthly trade deficit.

For you: Japanese cars, cameras and electronics are getting pricier at the source. Expect less discounting on imports into the holidays.

Covers China, Japan, Korea, Taiwan, Southeast Asia. Xinhua

India and South Asia

Goldman Sachs expects three more RBI rate hikes.

The repo rate is 5.50% after October's hike. Goldman sees it peaking at 6.25%, with one hike in December. The rupee is about 93.45 per dollar, up from 96.96 in May.

For you: Borrowing in India or sending money home? Loan rates are likely to rise, and the rupee has recovered from its lows.

Covers India, Pakistan, Bangladesh, Sri Lanka, Nepal. Business Today

Europe

The EU proposes a €45 billion loan to keep Ukraine running in 2027.

Ursula von der Leyen proposed €25 billion for military needs and €20 billion for the budget, about two thirds of Kyiv's expected €70 billion gap. All 27 members must agree, and Hungary, Slovakia, Czechia and Bulgaria are resisting.

For you: Defense spending keeps rising in Europe. European defense stocks and funds stay tied to these votes.

Covers European Union, United Kingdom, Ukraine, Russia. Euronews

Middle East

Iran hit a US radar site in Bahrain. The US struck Qeshm Island.

Iran then targeted an air base in Kuwait, which used its air defenses for the first time since early September. An ADNOC ship was targeted in the strait. No casualties were reported.

For you: Traveling through Gulf hubs? Check airline alerts before you leave and keep flexible bookings.

Covers Gulf states, Iran, Israel, Iraq, Yemen. Al Jazeera

Africa

South Africa's inflation jumped to 5.2%, its highest since May 2024.

It rose from 4.5% in September. Fuel prices rose 11.8% in the month and 42.9% over the year, and transport alone added 2.1 points to the headline rate.

For you: Sending rand or doing business in South Africa? Fuel costs are pushing prices up fast, which raises the odds of higher rates there.

Covers Nigeria, South Africa, Kenya, DR Congo, Ethiopia, Egypt. TimesLIVE

South America

Chile's economy shrank 1.1% in the third quarter.

Output fell 1.8% from the previous quarter, worse than forecast, as mining slumped. Polymarket traders give 62.5% odds the central bank holds in December (market odds).

For you: Chile is a top copper supplier. Weak mining output can lift prices for wiring, plumbing and electronics.

Covers Brazil, Argentina, Colombia, Chile, Peru. The Rio Times

Space

Voyager 1 is now one light-day from Earth, a first for any spacecraft.

It crossed the mark at 5:16 a.m. Eastern on November 18, about 16.09 billion miles away. A command and its reply now take two days.

For you: Technology built in the 1970s is still sending data. Public science investments can pay off for decades.

Medicine

FDA approves the first IL-17 drug for polymyalgia rheumatica.

Novartis's Cosentyx doubled sustained remission at one year in its trial, 40.6% versus 20.4% on placebo. About 966,000 people in the US have the condition.

For you: Have this condition or care for someone who does? Ask a doctor whether the new option fits, and check your plan's coverage.
