Stocks and your savings

Stocks have their best day since April 2025 as Nvidia jumps 31.5%.

High stakes for savers

Happened

On Monday the S&P 500 rose 326.78 points, or 4.7%, to 7,237.17, ending a seven day losing streak. The Dow rose 1,447.98 points, or 3%, to 49,595.31, the Nasdaq 6.5% to 24,089.56 and the Russell 2000 3.4% to 2,669.66, AP reports. Nvidia rose 31.5% after its board reinstated Jensen Huang as CEO. The VIX fell to 26.62.

Happening

Early Tuesday, Dow futures were up 0.1%, S&P 500 futures were near flat and Nasdaq 100 futures were down 0.3%, while Nvidia slipped 3% before the open, Yahoo Finance reported. Asia rallied: the KOSPI rose 6.5%, the Nikkei 3.9% and Taiwan more than 9%, while the Hang Seng lagged. Dell, HP, Best Buy, Medtronic, Analog Devices, Workday and Dollar Tree report earnings on Tuesday, and the Conference Board consumer confidence index is expected at 92.1, from 91.7. US markets close Thursday for Thanksgiving.

Could happen

A one day rally after a seven day slide does not settle the market's direction. Wednesday's PCE inflation report and the Fed's December decision matter more now.

FIOPO read. Judgment, not fact.

What it means for you

  • Saving for retirement? Keep your regular contributions on schedule; trying to time a 4.7% day is how investors miss them.
  • Rebalance only if your stock share has drifted well past your target, and do it on a set date, not after a headline.
  • Need the money within two years? Keep it in savings or short Treasury bills, not in stocks.

Iran war and oil

Iran's Revolutionary Guard says three tankers hit mines in Hormuz as Qatar works to revive talks.

High stakes for prices

Happened

Iran's Revolutionary Guard navy said on Monday that three tankers caught fire after hitting naval mines in the Strait of Hormuz, despite a US Navy escort, Iranian state media reported. It said US escorts would not exempt shipowners from its traffic rules. US Central Command had not responded, and the claim could not be independently verified.

Happening

Qatar's prime minister is working with Iran and the US to revive negotiations and met Vice President Vance and Secretary of State Rubio, Newsquawk reports. Iran wants sanctions lifted, frozen assets released and the US naval blockade removed; the US wants nuclear concessions. Brent fell 3.45% on Monday to $89.39, a three month low, and traded near $89 on Tuesday. Goldman Sachs raised its December Brent forecast to $105 from $90. The Houthis claimed an attack on Saudi Arabia's Rabigh refinery; Saudi officials have not confirmed damage.

The Fed and your loans

The Fed's Williams says inflation is still too high as traders bet on a December hike.

High stakes for borrowers

Happened

New York Fed President John Williams said on Monday that inflation is too high and the Fed must act over time to bring it back to 2%. He expects inflation to end 2026 at 3.5% and reach the target in 2028, and said he would not rule out moves at consecutive meetings. The Fed's rate range is 3.75% to 4.00%, Reuters reports.

Happening

Traders see about a 95% chance of a quarter point hike at the December meeting and a 5% chance of a half point, Yahoo Finance reported Tuesday. Governor Christopher Waller has signaled he could support a half point move, Reuters says. The 2 year Treasury yield rose to 4.683% and the dollar index hit a fresh annual high of 104.33 on Monday, IC Markets says. November's flash composite PMI fell to 54.6 from 55.9. Wednesday's PCE report is forecast to show inflation at 3.8% a year, up from 3.6%.

Market odds from futures pricing, Yahoo Finance, November 24. Not a FIOPO call.

What it means for you

  • Carrying a credit card balance? Rates follow the Fed; pay down the highest rate card first.
  • Shopping for a mortgage? Ask your lender about a rate lock before the December 9 decision.
  • Holding cash? Compare high yield savings and money market rates, which tend to rise after hikes.

Trade and chips

The US blacklists an Alibaba unit and six other firms linked to Megaspeed.

Medium stakes for investors

Happened

The Commerce Department added seven companies in Singapore, Malaysia and Thailand to its Entity List on Monday, including Megaspeed International and Apex Enterprise Solutions, Alibaba's Singapore subsidiary. It cited the risk of moving advanced AI chips to buyers the US has not approved. Export licenses for them will face a presumption of denial.

Happening

Alibaba said the listing is erroneous because Apex never diverted restricted chips, and that it will seek all legal avenues. Its Hong Kong shares were down 0.5% on Tuesday afternoon. Singapore Prime Minister Lawrence Wong said Singapore does not want to be used as a conduit to evade US controls and its investigation continues. Senators Maria Cantwell and Pete Ricketts said Commerce still owes Congress answers. The rule is due in the Federal Register on Wednesday.

Could happen

Alibaba's legal challenge and congressional probes could keep export controls in the headlines for weeks.

FIOPO read. Judgment, not fact.

What it means for you

  • Own Alibaba or a China tech fund? Expect headline swings; check how much of your fund sits in Chinese tech.
  • Buy cloud computing in Southeast Asia? Ask providers how US export rules affect their chip supply.
  • Work in tech trade or compliance? Entity List changes are a sign to review your customer screening.

Africa

Nigeria's central bank decides on rates today with the naira near 1,394.

Nigeria's Monetary Policy Committee ends its last meeting of 2026 on Tuesday, with the policy rate at 23% after a 350 basis point cut in September. Inflation eased to 14.06% in October from 14.63% in September, but food inflation is 19.13%. The naira closed Monday at 1,394.01 per dollar on the official market, its weakest close in about eight months, and reserves were $54.58 billion.

For you: Sending money to Nigeria? Compare official and transfer app rates; the gap to the street rate was about 51 naira on Monday.

South America

Brazil's lower house passes spending curbs as stocks hit a six month low.

Brazil's Chamber of Deputies approved the main text of a fiscal bill by 353 votes to 128 on Monday night. It would cap real spending growth at 2% instead of 2.5% in some years, and the government estimates a 2027 gain of R$45.1 billion to R$56.1 billion. The Ibovespa fell 0.95% to 164,796, 21.2% below its October 9 peak, and the dollar closed at 5.9306 reais. Petrobras raised its five year investment plan to $122 billion.

For you: Visiting Brazil? The real is near its weakest in over a year, so your dollars go further; pay in reais at card terminals.

Covers Brazil, Argentina, Chile, Colombia, Peru. The Rio Times, fiscal bill, Nov 24, The Rio Times, Brazil markets, Nov 24, Newsquawk, Nov 24

Football, England

Manchester United's revenue rises 4.1% but its quarterly loss grows.

In the quarter to September 30, revenue rose to £146.1 million from £140.3 million, while the wage bill rose 8.6% to £79.9 million. The after tax loss was £18.1 million, up 174.2%. Long term debt rose to £577.4 million after a refinancing, while interest payments fell 32% to £14.5 million.

For you: Own shares in a listed club? Watch wages against revenue; when wages grow faster, profits usually shrink.

Biology and medicine

Scientists find how microbes make drug-like molecules.

University of Michigan researchers reported in Science on November 23 that a protein family called REACT lets bacteria and fungi build complex drug-like molecules, with more than 40,000 related proteins identified.

For you: Follow biotech? Discoveries like this can shorten the search for new antibiotics, though new drugs take years to reach pharmacies.
